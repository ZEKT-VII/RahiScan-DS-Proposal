\section{The Case Study: A Morning on Raiwind Road}

\begin{minipage}[t]{0.57\textwidth}\vspace{0pt}
It is 7:40 on a Monday in Johar Town and we have an 8:30 lab at \textbf{Superior University Gold Campus} on Raiwind Road (\(9.6\,\mathrm{km}\)). The first \textbf{Qingqi rickshaw} quotes 140 rupees; we bargain down to 110 and board. The driver then crawls along the left lane, stops for tea at a roadside dhaba ``for one minute'', and delivers us twelve minutes late. The next morning a different Qingqi driver covers the same \(9.6\,\mathrm{km}\) route to Superior University Gold Campus for the standard \textbf{80 rupees} in twenty minutes despite heavier traffic (similarly, the \(16.1\,\mathrm{km}\) ride from \textbf{Raiwind} to Gold Campus costs about \textbf{120 rupees}, and inner-city Lahore routes average 40--85 rupees).

Lahore's Qingqi motorcycle-rickshaws lack meters, leaving commuters unable to tell at the curb whether a quote is inflated or whether a driver is habitually slow. We propose \textbf{RahiScan}, a crowd-sourced mobile app where passengers photograph a Qingqi rickshaw before boarding (Figure~\ref{fig:hero}), fusing number-plate OCR with driver facial recognition (as vehicles are shared across shifts), enter the quoted fare, and log 5-second GPS pings until arrival.
\end{minipage}\hfill

\section{Define: Turning Frustration into Measurable Targets}
\stagebanner{1}{DEFINE}{What problem should be solved?}{navyblue}

To replace subjective complaints with testable metrics, we define two dimensionless ratios for every Qingqi ride \(i\), where \(1.0\) represents typical behaviour on that corridor and hour, and fix badge thresholds prior to data collection (Table~\ref{tab:define}):

\begin{tcolorbox}[enhanced, colback=white, colframe=navyblue, boxrule=0.7pt, arc=2pt, sidebyside, sidebyside align=top, lefthand width=0.52\textwidth, left=5pt, right=5pt, top=1.5pt, bottom=1.5pt, before skip=1.5pt, after skip=2pt]
{\color{navyblue}\bfseries\footnotesize Driving Pace Index (\(\Pi_i\))}
\[ \textstyle \Pi_i = \frac{v_{\mathrm{obs},i}}{v_{\mathrm{base},i}} = \frac{d_i / t_i}{\operatorname{median}(v_{\mathrm{obs}}\text{ on corridor, hour})} \]
{\scriptsize \(\Pi=0.60\) means moving 40\% slower than peer Qingqi rickshaws on the same road and hour.}
\tcblower
{\color{accentorange}\bfseries\footnotesize Fare Fairness Ratio (\(\Phi_i\))}
\[ \textstyle \Phi_i = \frac{\mathrm{Fare}_{\mathrm{quoted},i}}{\widehat{\mathrm{Fare}}_{\mathrm{fair},i}} \]
{\scriptsize \(\widehat{\mathrm{Fare}}_{\mathrm{fair}}\) is predicted from distance, corridor, and congestion (\(\approx 80\) PKR for \(9.6\,\mathrm{km}\); \(\approx 120\) PKR Raiwind to Gold Campus).}
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{Clean: Can the Data Be Trusted?}
\stagebanner{3}{CLEAN}{Can the data be trusted?}{accentorange}

Street telemetry contains four recurring defects that we injected into our mock pilot and resolved programmatically. First, \textbf{duplicate uploads} from cellular retries within 10 seconds are dropped (\(1,224 \to 1,200\)). Second, \textbf{missing or typo fares} (e.g.\ 800 instead of 80 PKR) outside \([0.4, 4.0]\times\) benchmark are removed (\(1,200 \to 1,151\)). Third, \textbf{night glare and dented plates} degrade OCR; requiring plate confidence \(\ge 0.85\) or face similarity \(\ge 0.70\) (and using the face to disambiguate shared plates) boosts identity precision from 83.5\% to \textbf{99.8\%} (\(1,151 \to 1,090\)), leaving \textbf{1,071 verified rides} after dropping 19 underpass traces with \(<75\%\) GPS coverage (Figure~\ref{fig:clean_pair}, left). Fourth, when a student forgets to tap \emph{End Ride}, our backward change-point filter trims trailing pings with speed \(<6\,\mathrm{km/h}\) and step variance \(>1.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\) for \(\ge 60\,\mathrm{s}\), catching \textbf{82.4\%} of all 148 walking tails at \textbf{100\% precision} and restoring \(2.36\,\mathrm{km/h}\) to affected drivers (Figure~\ref{fig:clean_pair}, right).

\section{Model: From Patterns to Predictions}
\stagebanner{5}{MODEL}{Can patterns support prediction?}{accentblue}

Our modelling architecture operates in two tiers (Figure~\ref{fig:model_pair}, left). \textbf{Tier 1 (Biometric + OCR Identity Fusion)} pairs a CRNN plate reader with a MobileNetV3 128-D face encoder. Because six Qingqi rickshaws in our pilot are shared across shifts, a single plate is often driven by a fair morning driver (\(\Phi \approx 1.02\)) and an overpriced evening driver (\(\Phi \approx 1.75\)) who would otherwise be blurred into one misleading profile. \textbf{Tier 2 (Behavioural Prediction)} trains a Gradient-Boosted Regressor (GBR) to predict \(\widehat{\mathrm{Fare}}_{\mathrm{fair}}\) from distance, corridor, hour, weekend indicator, and ambient speed \(v_{\mathrm{base}}\), plus a multiclass GBR pace classifier. Plotting 4-week driver medians reveals four behavioural clusters and confirms that pace and pricing require separate badges (Figure~\ref{fig:model_pair}, right).

\section{Evaluate: How Reliable Is the Result?}
\stagebanner{6}{EVALUATE}{How reliable is the result?}{navyblue}

To prevent temporal leakage, models are trained strictly on Weeks 1--3 (808 clean rides) and evaluated on the unseen Week-4 holdout (263 rides across all 40 drivers; Table~\ref{tab:eval}). Fair-fare GBR achieves a Mean Absolute Error of \textbf{1.8 PKR} against a \(<5\) PKR target (beating distance-only linear regression at 2.6 PKR and a flat median rate at 22.5 PKR). Single-ride pace reaches macro \(F_1 = \mathbf{0.71}\) (below the 0.88 target, vs.\ 0.54 for raw speed) because individual trips suffer from one-off traffic luck, whereas aggregating over \(\ge 5\) rides eliminates single-trip noise and lifts driver-level Pace Badge accuracy to \textbf{100\%} (versus 90.0\% using raw speed; Figure~\ref{fig:eval_pair}).

\section{Deploy: How Will the Decision Be Used?}
\stagebanner{7}{DEPLOY}{How will the decision be used?}{accentgreen}

The evaluated pipeline will run behind a lightweight FastAPI service powering a three-screen mobile workflow (Figure~\ref{fig:deploy_pair}, left): (1) curbside AR camera scan of the Qingqi rickshaw's plate and driver face, (2) live 5-second GPS trip telemetry along Raiwind Road and inner-city corridors with automatic walking-tail detection, and (3) an instant curbside scorecard returned within \textbf{two seconds} so the student can negotiate (e.g.\ comparing a 140 PKR quote against the 80 PKR fair fare) or wave the Qingqi on before boarding.

Because our Evaluate stage proved that single rides are too noisy to judge a driver fairly (\(F_1 = 0.71\)), deployment enforces four operational safeguards (Table~\ref{tab:safeguards}). First, the app displays \emph{Insufficient Rides (\(<5\))} until a driver accumulates at least five verified trips, where badge accuracy reaches 100\%. Second, driver scores use an exponentially weighted moving median that weights recent rides more heavily, allowing drivers who improve their pricing or pace to recover their badge. Third, drivers can scan their own plate to inspect their log and flag disputed trips for review. Fourth, because fuel prices and inflation shift over time, the fair-fare GBR model is retrained monthly against fresh commuter benchmarks, feeding every completed trip back into the \textbf{Collect} stage. Figure~\ref{fig:deploy_pair} (right) outlines our eight-week execution plan from prototype to Superior University Gold Campus beta.

\begin{insight}[Conclusion: Closing the Lifecycle Loop]
Our morning Qingqi commute to Superior University Gold Campus exposed a concrete failure mode at every stage of the Data Science Lifecycle. By validating our design on a 1,224-trip simulated Lahore pilot, we demonstrated that combining plate OCR with facial embeddings resolves shared-vehicle ambiguity at 99.8\% precision, change-point trimming removes forgotten walking tails, gradient boosting predicts fair Qingqi fares (\(\approx 80\) PKR for \(9.6\,\mathrm{km}\) and \(\approx 120\) PKR from Raiwind to Gold Campus) within 1.8 PKR, and traffic-normalised multi-ride medians classify driver pace and pricing at 100\% accuracy. Replacing our mock CSV streams with live student telemetry will turn RahiScan into a practical transparency tool for Lahore's daily commuters.
\end{insight}
